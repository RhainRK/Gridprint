    How it works

    The grid never sits still

    Britain’s electricity runs at about 50 Hz, but it drifts by a few hundredths of a hertz every second as demand rises and falls. The whole country shares the same drift, and the grid operator publishes it.

    Microphones hear it

    Anything plugged in hums faintly at the grid’s frequency. Phones, laptops and cameras record that hum, even when you can’t hear it.

    Gridprint matches it

    It reads the drift in the hum, finds the one moment in the grid’s record where it fits, and checks that the recording runs unbroken. Nemotron runs the investigation and explains the result.

    Gridprint needs hum to work. When a recording has none, it says so rather than guessing, because missing hum proves nothing on its own.
